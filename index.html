02 What it does

No integrations.
It just uses the app.

Assistants fail because they need every app to build them a door. AURA doesn't ask for a door — it reads the screen and uses the app the way a person does.

Perception

It reads the screen two ways at once

One pass reads Android's own accessibility data — exact, fast, ground truth. A second pass runs a vision model and OCR on your phone, catching what the first can't see: maps, games, web views, custom-drawn buttons. The two are merged, so a button that's invisible to one is still found by the other.

Typing

It types in the apps that normally block automation

Rapido, Swiggy, Uber and most delivery and ride apps are built with React Native or Flutter — they have no standard Android text field, so ordinary automation types into nothing and silently fails. AURA ships its own invisible keyboard, switches to it only to enter your text, then switches straight back. It never reads what you type.

Voice

Say "Hey AURA"

Wake word, speech detection and playback run on the phone. Interrupt it mid-sentence and it stops talking and listens — like a person would.

Shortcuts

One call, not ten taps

Alarms, timers, calls, texts, calendar events, directions and sharing go straight through Android instead of opening an app and hunting for buttons.

Notifications

Answers without opening anything

"What did Ravi say?" — it reads the shade and can reply, dismiss, or pause your music from there. Banking and security notifications are filtered out first.

Your key, your model

Bring your own brain

Pick Groq, OpenRouter or Google Gemini and paste your own API key — stored encrypted on your phone, sent only to the provider you chose. There is no AURA account, no AURA server, and no subscription. Swap models whenever you like.

Memory

It gets better at your apps

When AURA finds the route to something, it keeps the route — not the contents. A privacy filter strips personal details before anything is written down, and everything it remembers is encrypted on the device. You can read it, and wipe it, from Settings.

You can type instead of talking, and you can stop a task at any time from the bubble. If AURA can't do something it says so — it won't quietly report success for a task that didn't happen.

04 How it works

Look. Act. Look again.

The reason most phone automation breaks is that it assumes the tap worked. AURA checks. Every action is followed by a fresh look at the screen, and if the screen didn't change the way it expected, it tries something else.

STEP 1 Perceive

Capture the screen, find every element that can be touched, and give each one a number. Nothing is estimated from pixels.

STEP 2 Act

Choose a number and a verb — tap 5, type into 4, scroll down. Where a direct route exists, take it instead: a deep link beats four taps.

STEP 3 Verify

Look again and confirm the screen actually changed. If it didn't, that's a failure — not a success to report and move on from.

Back to step 1, until the goal is met or AURA tells you it couldn't

05 Safety

You're handing it your phone. Here's what it can't do.

An assistant that can tap anything is only trustworthy if some things are permanently off the table. These limits are enforced in code, before any action runs — the AI cannot talk its way past them.

Hard block
Banking apps stay shut

Banking, payment and authenticator apps are on a blocklist. AURA refuses to open them or follow a link into them, and tells you why.

Hard block
It won't type your secrets

Card numbers, CVVs, national ID numbers and anything shaped like "my password is…" are rejected before a single keystroke is sent.

Every tool
One gate, no side doors

Every one of the 47 tools goes through the same checkpoint — permission, policy, audit log. There is no path that skips it, including for the AI's own calls.

Human in the loop
It asks instead of guessing

When a choice is genuinely yours — which address, which of three results — AURA stops and asks, out loud, rather than picking one and hoping.

On device
Perception never leaves the phone

Reading the screen, detecting elements and recognising text all run locally. Your chosen AI model receives only what it needs to decide the next step.

Receipts
You can read the whole log

Every tool call AURA makes is recorded on the device and readable in the app — what it did, when, and whether it worked.

Honest limits. AURA needs Android's accessibility service to see and touch the screen — the same permission a screen reader uses. It reads screen content only while running a task you asked for. AI can still misread a screen, so review anything that spends money or can't be undone.

07 Questions

The things people ask first

Does AURA watch my screen all the time?

No. It reads the screen while it is carrying out something you asked for, and stops when the task ends. Reading the screen happens on the phone; it is your AI provider that receives whatever is needed to decide the next step, and only for that step.

What does it cost?

The app is free and there's no account to create. You bring your own AI provider key — Groq has a free tier that's enough to try it, and Google's Gemini free tier works too. You pay your provider directly, if at all. Nothing is billed by AURA.

Why is the download 221 MB?

Because the vision models that read your screen ship inside the app rather than living on a server. That's the trade: a bigger download once, in exchange for perception that runs on your phone.

This build also carries code for three processor types in one file so a single APK works everywhere. Splitting it per processor would cut the size to roughly a third and is planned.

Why isn't it on the Play Store?

It's coming. For now AURA is distributed directly so it can ship and improve quickly. That means you install it yourself — the download page walks through every step, including the one Android hides.

Which phones does it work on?

Android 8.0 or newer, on Arm or x86. Xiaomi, OPPO, realme, vivo, OnePlus, Samsung, Huawei, Honor, Pixel and Motorola are all supported — though some of those add extra permission switches of their own, which the download page covers per brand.

Does it update itself?

Not automatically — apps installed outside the Play Store can't. AURA shows a banner when a new version is out and links you here. Updates install straight over the old app; you keep your settings, memory and keys.

Can it work without internet?

Partly. Seeing the screen, hearing the wake word and speaking back all work offline. Deciding what to do next needs your AI provider, which needs a connection.

What happens if it does the wrong thing?

You can stop a run at any time from the floating bubble or the notification. Every step is logged in the app so you can see exactly what it did. AURA refuses banking and payment apps outright, and asks before anything genuinely ambiguous — but it is AI, it can be wrong, and anything irreversible deserves your eyes.
