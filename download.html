02 Install

Five steps, in order

Android treats apps from outside the Play Store carefully. Nothing here is a workaround — it's the normal path, it's just spread across screens that are easy to miss. Step 4 is the one everybody gets stuck on.

Download the file on your phone

Tap the download button above from the phone itself, not from your computer. The file is called aura-1.0.358.apk. It's large, so use Wi-Fi if you can.

Allow installs from your browser

When you open the downloaded file, Android says the browser isn't allowed to install apps. Tap Settings in that message and turn on the switch — it grants permission to whichever app you downloaded with, not to the whole system.

Settings›Apps›Special app access›Install unknown apps›Chrome

You can switch it back off after installing.

Get past Play Protect

Google scans apps it hasn't seen before and shows "Unsafe app blocked" or "App scan recommended". This is Play Protect flagging an unknown developer, not a detection of anything.

Tap More details → Install anyway. If you'd rather verify first, check the file's fingerprint against the one published below.

Allow restricted settings — the one everyone misses

On Android 13 and newer, apps installed outside the Play Store are blocked from using the Accessibility service. AURA needs it to see and touch the screen, so without this step the app installs but can't do anything. When you try to enable it you'll see a dialog reading "Restricted setting — for your security, this setting is currently unavailable."

Do this before opening AURA's setup. Go to app info and unlock restricted settings first — otherwise onboarding sends you to a switch you're not allowed to flip yet, which reads like the app is broken.
Settings›Apps›AURA›⋮ (top right)›Allow restricted settings

Your phone may ask for your PIN, pattern or fingerprint — that's expected. Some brands word the menu item "Allow restricted setting" or place it behind "More". If you don't see it at all, your Android version doesn't need it.

Open AURA and follow setup

AURA asks for what it needs, one screen at a time, and explains each one before the system dialog appears. It needs four things to work at all — microphone, the floating bubble, the accessibility service, and its own keyboard — and asks for everything else only when a task actually needs it.

Last, add an AI provider key in Settings → Agent Brain. Groq and Google Gemini both have free tiers that are enough to try it.

03 Your phone brand

Some brands add their own switches

Xiaomi, OPPO, vivo and Huawei layer extra permission gates on top of Android's. The tell-tale symptom is that everything looks enabled and the floating bubble still never appears. Find your brand below.

Xiaomi · Redmi · POCO MIUI / HyperOS

MIUI has a second, private overlay permission that Android's own switch doesn't cover. The app can't detect or request it — it just silently never draws. This is the most common "the bubble doesn't show up" report.

  1. Open Settings → Apps → Manage apps → AURA
  2. Tap Other permissions
  3. Turn ON Display pop-up windows while running in background
  4. Also turn ON Display pop-up windows if it is listed
  5. Back on the app page, turn ON Autostart — without it AURA won't survive a reboot
OPPO · realme ColorOS
  1. Open Settings → Apps → AURA → Allow floating windows
  2. Turn ON Allow floating windows and Display over other apps
  3. Open Settings → Battery → App battery usage → AURA → Allow background activity
  4. In the Phone Manager app, enable Auto-launch for AURA
vivo · iQOO Funtouch / OriginOS
  1. Open Settings → Apps → AURA → Permissions → Floating windows → Allow
  2. Open i Manager → App manager → Autostart manager and turn ON AURA
  3. Open Settings → Battery → High background power consumption and allow AURA
Huawei · Honor EMUI / MagicOS
  1. Open Settings → Apps → AURA → Permissions and turn ON Display over other apps
  2. Open Settings → Battery → App launch → AURA and switch to Manage manually
  3. Enable all three: Auto-launch, Secondary launch, Run in background
OnePlus OxygenOS

The standard Android switches are enough for the bubble to appear. One extra step keeps AURA alive between sessions:

  1. Open Settings → Battery → Battery optimisation → AURA and choose Don't optimise
  2. If your build has it, allow AURA in Settings → Apps → App auto-launch
Samsung One UI

No extra overlay permission needed. One UI is aggressive about sleeping apps, though:

  1. Open Settings → Battery → Background usage limits
  2. Make sure AURA is not in Sleeping apps or Deep sleeping apps
  3. Open Settings → Apps → AURA → Battery and choose Unrestricted
Pixel · Motorola · Nothing · stock Android nothing extra

Nothing beyond the five install steps above. Stock Android has no hidden overlay gate, so once the accessibility service is on, AURA works. Do make sure Restricted settings is unlocked — step 4 applies to every brand.

04 Permissions

What it asks for, and why

Four permissions are needed for AURA to function at all. Everything else is asked for the first time a task actually needs it, and refusing simply removes that ability. You can revoke any of them later in Android settings.

Permission When Why AURA needs it
Microphone Required Hear your voice commands and talk with you.
Display over other apps Required Show the chat bubble on top of whatever you're doing.
Accessibility service Required See the screen and perform taps, swipes and typing for you. AURA reads on-screen content only while running your tasks; banking and payment apps are always blocked.
AURA Keyboard Required Lets AURA type in every app. Many ride, delivery and food apps are built on React Native or Flutter and have no standard Android text field, so the normal typing path silently fails there. AURA's keyboard has no visible keys and never reads what you type — it switches to it only to enter your text, then switches straight back.
Notifications Required Keep you posted on task progress while AURA works in the background.
Contacts When needed Find a contact's number on-device when you say "message Amma". Numbers never leave your phone.
Notification access When needed Read and act on notifications — reply to messages, control media. Banking and security notifications are filtered out and never shown to the agent.
Photos, media & files When needed Search and open your files when you ask — "open the PDF I downloaded".
Screen capture When needed Take screenshots so the agent can see screens the accessibility data can't describe. Asked once per session, the first time it's needed.
Camera When needed Use the camera and flashlight when a task needs them.
Location When needed Answer "where am I" and share your location when you ask.
Alarms & reminders When needed Set alarms and timers exactly when you ask for them.
Unrestricted battery When needed Keep long tasks running when the screen is off.

06 Known limits

What this build can't do yet

  • No automatic updates. Apps installed outside the Play Store can't update themselves. AURA shows a banner when a new version exists and links back here.
  • Same network only for the developer MCP feature. Your phone and computer must be on the same Wi-Fi — connecting across networks needs a relay that isn't set up yet.
  • Large download. One universal file carrying on-device models and three processor architectures. Per-processor builds are planned and will cut it to roughly a third.
  • Not on the Play Store yet. Which is why the install has five steps instead of one.

07 If something's wrong

Three symptoms, three causes

The bubble never appears

Almost always a brand-specific overlay gate rather than Android's. The permission reads as granted because Android thinks it is — your manufacturer added a second switch behind it. Find your brand in the section above; Xiaomi, OPPO, vivo, Huawei and Honor all do this.

Accessibility won't turn on

That's Restricted settings — step 4. Go to Settings → Apps → AURA → ⋮ → Allow restricted settings, confirm with your PIN, then try the accessibility switch again. The switch will simply refuse until you do.

AURA stops working after a while, or after restarting

Battery management killed it. Set AURA's battery usage to Unrestricted (or Don't optimise), and on Xiaomi, OPPO, vivo, Huawei and Honor also enable Autostart / Auto-launch — that's the switch that decides whether it survives a reboot.

Still stuck

Open Settings → Permissions health inside the app — it shows exactly which permission is missing and takes you straight to the right screen. If that doesn't explain it, email dinesh210805@gmail.com with your phone model and Android version.
